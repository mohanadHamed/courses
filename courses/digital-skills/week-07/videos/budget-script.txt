A monthly budget in Excel.
Sami plans the money of one month.

A budget has income: the money that comes in.
It also has expenses: the money that goes out.
Sami makes two blocks on one sheet.
Income is at the top, and expenses are below.
Each block ends with a total line.

The amounts are in column B.
The cells hold only numbers.
JD goes in the heading of the column, not in the cells.

Now Excel adds up the money.
The total income is the sum of B2 to B3: 250 JD.
The total expenses are the sum of B7 to B11: 195 JD.
What is left is the income minus the expenses.
250 minus 195 leaves 55 JD.

Each expense is a part of the income.
Sami divides each amount by the total income.
Two dollar signs keep the total income in every row.
Food is 90 of 250, so its part is 0.36.

A number format changes the look, not the value.
In the Percentage format, 0.36 looks like 36%.
Money gets two decimals, so 55 looks like 55.00.
The formula bar still shows the real value.

If one amount changes, every total changes by itself.
