# Digital Media Technologies and Creative Production for Games (13411134) — course notes

Course-specific rules only. Global rules are in the root `AUTHORING.md`.

## Timing and textbook

- 2 credit hours (1 theory + 1 seminar), taught every week as one lecture
  of 60 min and one seminar of 60 min; the seminar practises the lecture of
  the same week (analysis, critique, production practice).
- Textbook: Schell, *The Art of Game Design: A Book of Lenses*, 3rd ed.
  References: Collins, *Game Sound* (2008); McKee, *Story* (1997).
- PLO 2 (player experience) is behind almost every ILO: always tie a media
  element to its effect on the player.
- Size for **60 minutes** (3–4 sections). Every lesson ends with a ~10-min
  Game challenge that each student does alone.

## Media in lessons

- Sound demos are generated in the browser with Web Audio (no audio files
  from the internet). Start sound only after a tap.
- Example games are invented (Lantern Keeper, Ember Trail); their screens are
  drawn as SVG illustrations. Never use screenshots, music or video of
  commercial games.
- Production weeks (audio 7–10, video 11–12) need free tools that run in the
  lab (Audacity, DaVinci Resolve): check with me before relying on a tool.

- 2026-11-04 (teacher's review of Seminar 2): the story sessions asked the
  students, and even the teacher, to compose: a premise of their own idea,
  a scene in screenplay format, rewritten lines, barks, a music brief,

  no right answer, and no ILO asks for it: A1 explains (Bloom 2), B1

  media (3), B2 evaluates (5); no ILO is at level 6 (create), and PLO 2 is

  order shuffled stages, cards or lines, find the missing part, choose
  among A, B and C, classify given notes. The production tasks stay

  checked with a checklist or by the simulator. The syllabus titles stay:
  "Drafting a Story Premise" builds a premise from five given cards;
  "Writing a Scene and Its Dialogue" puts the shuffled lines of a scene in
  screenplay format. Tasks on "a game you know" became fixed games (Harbor
  Run, Sky Kite, Market Day, Snow Post, Bubble Farm). Fix: in Mix Desk the
  first mix does not clip (the old Seminar 9 answer said it did). The team
  activities of Practice 1 and 2 (D1) stay. Lecture 1, Seminar 1 and
  Lecture 2 were already taught and did not change.
- 2026-11-04 (teacher's review, second round): Seminar 2 was still hard for
  a first reader. Its page showed the list of stages as a broken numbered
  list ("2. Dark water"), the tension numbers fell at the start (6, 5, 4)
  against the rising curve of Lecture 2, and the cards of Stage Order said
  other things than the text. From Seminar 2 on, every session now follows
  these clarity rules, with the same ILOs and the same activities:
  - The facts of an activity are a numbered table (stages, lines, cards,
    notes, assets), never an inline list, and no source line starts with
    a number or a letter (Pandoc turns it into a list).
  - A number always comes with its name: "stage 2 (The alarm)".
  - Every number or scale is explained the first time (tension: 1 = very
    calm, 10 = very tense; 0 dB is the top; 48 kHz = 48 000 samples a
    second).
  - Recall has a small table of the words of the lecture in plain English.
  - In a match, the first answer is done as an example; every step is one
    action; choices are a list (A, B, C); answers go in a table column.
  - The slides have a "The facts · …" table slide for the data of each
    activity; long columns get their width from the dashes of the table.
  - A simulator shows exactly the names and numbers of the text (Moon
    Diver: stage 1 The base, stage 2 The alarm; tension 3, 4, 5, 2, 7, 8,
    10, 2), with a new poster and recording.
